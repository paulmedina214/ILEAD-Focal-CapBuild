Summary and key findings

This dashboard presents the baseline results of the Capacity and Competency Assessment Tool (CCAT), completed by all 36 I-LEAD focal persons across seven offices in the Central Office and Regions XI and XII, under Memorandum Order No. CO.OED.26.03.217. It assesses staff across 93 skills grouped into 11 competency clusters. Results are analyzed by position family rather than office, because averaging by office lets the high scores of leadership mask the urgent needs of frontline staff.

1
A strong, workable baseline. The overall mean is 3.19 of 5 (Working Knowledge). This is a capable team that understands its core duties; the task is to lift them toward independent and expert level, especially in the newer areas the I-LEAD Project introduces, not to train from scratch.
2
The gap is a transfer gap, not an absence. Management and Leadership average 3.89 with no cluster below the line, while frontline delivery staff fall below Working Knowledge in most clusters (Technical Assistants 2.83, Technical Specialists 2.85, Development Management Officers 2.89). The expertise exists; it has not yet reached the field.
3
Two clusters are weakest and uncovered. Digital/AI/Knowledge Management (2.97) and CDF/Enterprise/Livelihood (2.99) have both the widest demand (16 and 18 people below 3.0) and no internal volunteer mentor. These are the clearest case for recruiting or commissioning external support.
4
Internal expertise can bridge most gaps. Seven senior staff volunteered as mentors, matched to 24 mentees by shared gap, with 17 more offering to help. A Peer-to-Practice (P2P) approach can carry most of the Year 1 response, prioritizing the lowest-scoring clusters first.
5
Staff want applied, hands-on learning. Comments repeatedly ask for practical, on-the-job training, templates, and seminars over generic sessions. The training design should be targeted, practical, and role-specific.
